## 2. NARRATION STYLE

**THIS IS NOT A NARRATED DOCUMENTARY** - It's a **cinematic drama with dialogue**

### Characteristics:
- **Zero voice-over narration** - Story told entirely through:
  - Character dialogue
  - Visual storytelling
  - Environmental sound
  - Musical score
- **Dialogue-driven**: Every scene advances through conversation
- **Archaic/formal English** (translated from Arabic context): "I've called till my throat bleeds", "On his own two feet", "A man does not weigh a debt like that on a scale"
- **Poetic, quotable lines**: Designed to be memorable
- **Silence as tool**: Long pauses, meaningful looks, environmental sounds

### Speaking Patterns:
- **Meccan elite**: Formal, arrogant, measured, persuasive (Abu Jahl, Abu Lahab)
- **Muhammad's followers**: Sincere, direct, emotionally raw
- **Slaves/oppressed**: Quiet power, minimal words, maximum impact ("Ahad")
- **Ali**: Few words, each weighted, grows from child to warrior
- **Abu Talib**: Wise, protective, metaphorical ("lion", "high one")

## 3. VISUAL STYLE (Inferred from Script)

### Cinematic Language:
- **Period authenticity**: 7th century Mecca/Medina - markets, courtyards, Kaaba, desert
- **Lighting**: 
  - Warm golden for intimate/family scenes (Abu Talib's house)
  - Harsh sunlight for public torture (Bilal on sand)

  - Earth tones: ochre, sand, terracotta, dark browns
  - White/cream for Muhammad's followers (purity)
  - Dark reds/blacks for opposition
  - Gold accents for Kaaba, prestige

### Camera Work (Inferred):
- **Establishing shots**: Mecca valley, Kaaba, market streets
- **Intimate close-ups**: Eyes, hands, facial reactions
- **Low angles**: For powerful figures (Abu Jahl on steps, Amr on horse)
- **High angles**: For vulnerable (Bilal on ground, child Ali)
- **Tracking shots**: Following Ali delivering invitations through Mecca
- **Static composed frames**: Dialogue scenes, theatrical blocking

### Visual Consistency:
- **Character continuity**: Same actors, aging makeup, consistent costumes
- **Location continuity**: Recognizable sets (Abu Talib's house, Kaaba steps, market)
- **Costume language**: Distinct for each class (elite, common, slaves, Muhammad's followers)

## 4. EDITING RHYTHM

### Scene Duration:
- **Dialogue scenes**: 2-5 minutes (theatrical, let breathe)
- **Montage sequences**: 30-60 seconds (Ali running invitations, conversions growing)
- **Action beats**: 1-2 minutes (torture, combat)
- **Emotional beats**: Held longer (silence, reaction shots)

### Transitions:
- **Hard cuts**: Between locations/time jumps
- **Match cuts**: "Eleven years earlier" / "Eleven years later"
- **Audio bridges**: Dialogue carries over scene changes
- **Thematic parallels**: Child Ali learning sword ↔ Adult Ali in combat

### Pacing:
- **Slow burn first half**: Character setup, world-building
- **Accelerating middle**: Persecution escalates, conversions cascade
- **Climax**: Battle of Trench - tension builds to single combat
- **Resolution**: Philosophical dialogue, teaching moment

## 5. SOUND DESIGN

### Music:
- **Orchestral score**: Middle Eastern instruments (oud, ney, percussion)
- **Leitmotifs**: 
  - Ali's theme (courage, rising)
  - Muhammad's theme (reverence, subtle)
  - Opposition theme (menacing, heavy)
  - Faith theme (Bilal's "Ahad" - minimal, powerful)
- **Silence**: Strategic - before key lines, during torture, after emotional blows

### Ambient Sound:
- **Market**: Voices, animals, merchants calling
- **Desert**: Wind, sand shifting
- **Interior**: Fire crackling, tea pouring, breathing
- **Battle**: Metal on metal, horses, shouts, breathing

### Voice Acting:
- **No narrator** - all diegetic
- **Arabic terms preserved**: "Ahad", "Messenger of Allah", "Quraysh", "Hashim"
- **Emotional authenticity**: Cracking voices, whispers, shouted declarations
